[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 1] Jesus began to speak to them in parables, ‘A man once planted a vineyard, put a fence round it, dug a wine-press, built a tower, and then let it out to tenants and went abroad.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 2] At the proper time he sent a servant to the tenants, to receive from them a share of the produce of the grape harvest;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 3] but they seized him, and beat him, and sent him away empty-handed.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 4] A second time the owner sent a servant to them; this man, too, the tenants struck on the head, and insulted.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 5] He sent another, but him they killed; and so with many others – some they beat and some they killed.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 6] He had still one son, who was very dear to him; and him he sent to them last of all. “They will respect my son,” he said.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 7] But those tenants said to one another “Here is the heir! Come, let us kill him, and his inheritance will be ours.”
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 8] So they seized him, and killed him, and threw his body outside the vineyard.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 9] What will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come and put the tenants to death, and he will let the vineyard to others.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 10] ‘Have you never read this passage of scripture? – “The stone which the builders despised has now itself become the cornerstone;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 11] this cornerstone has come from the Lord, and is marvellous in our eyes.”’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 12] After this his enemies were eager to arrest him, but they were afraid of the crowd; for they saw that it was at them that he had aimed the parable. So they left him alone, and went away.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 13] Afterwards they sent to Jesus some of the Pharisees and Herodians, to set a trap for him in the course of conversation.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 14] These men came to him and said, ‘Teacher, we know that you are an honest man, and are not afraid of anyone, for you pay no regard to a person’s position, but teach the way of God honestly; are we right in paying taxes to the Emperor, or not?
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 15] Should we pay, or should we not pay?’ Knowing their hypocrisy, Jesus said to them, ‘Why are you testing me? Bring me a coin to look at.’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 16] And, when they had brought it, he asked, ‘Whose head and title are these?’ ‘The Emperor’s,’ they said;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 17] and Jesus replied, ‘Pay to the Emperor what belongs to the Emperor, and to God what belongs to God.’ And they were amazed at him.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 18] Next came some Sadducees – the men who maintain that there is no resurrection. Their question was this –
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 19] ‘Teacher, in our scriptures Moses decreed that, should a man’s brother die, leaving a widow but no child, the man should take the widow as his wife, and raise up a family for his brother.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 20] There were once seven brothers. The eldest married, but died and left no family;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 21] and the second married his widow, and died without family; and so did the third.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 22] All the seven died and left no family. The woman herself died last of all.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 23] At the resurrection whose wife will she be, all seven brothers having had her as their wife?’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 24] ‘Is not the reason of your mistake,’ answered Jesus, ‘your ignorance of the scriptures and of the power of God?
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 25] When people rise from the dead, there is no marrying or being married; but they are as angels in heaven.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 26] ‘As to the dead, and the fact that they rise, have you never read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the Bush, how God spoke to him saying – “I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob”?
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 27] He is not God of dead people, but of living. You are greatly mistaken.’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 28] Then came up one of the teachers of the Law who had heard their discussions. Knowing that Jesus had answered them wisely, he asked him this question, ‘Which commandment is the most important of all?’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 29] ‘The most important,’ answered Jesus, ‘is – “Hear, Israel; the Lord our God is the one Lord;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 30] and you must love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.”
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 31] The second is this – “You must love your neighbour as you love yourself.” There is no commandment greater than these.’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 32] ‘Wisely answered, teacher!’ exclaimed the teacher of the Law. ‘It is true, as you say, that there is one God, and that there is no other besides him;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 33] and to love him with all one’s heart, and with all one’s understanding, and with all one’s strength, and to love one’s neighbour as one loves oneself is far beyond all burnt offerings and sacrifices.’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 34] Seeing that he had answered with discernment, Jesus said to him, ‘You are not far from the kingdom of God.’ After that no one ventured to question him further.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 35] While Jesus was teaching in the Temple Courts, he asked, ‘How is it that the teachers of the Law say that the Christ is to be David’s son?
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 36] David said himself, speaking under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit – “The Lord said to my lord: Sit at my right hand, until I put your enemies beneath your feet.”
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 37] David himself calls him “lord,” how comes it, then, that he is to be his son?’ The mass of the people listened to Jesus with delight.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 38] In the course of his teaching, Jesus said, ‘See that you are on your guard against the teachers of the Law, who delight to walk about in long robes, and to be greeted in the streets with respect,
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 39] and to have the best seats in the synagogues, and places of honour at dinner.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 40] They are the men who rob widows of their homes, and make a pretence of saying long prayers. Their sentence will be all the heavier.’
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 41] Then Jesus sat down opposite the chests for the Temple offerings, and watched how the people put money into them. Many rich people were putting in large sums;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 42] but one poor widow came and put in two small coins, worth very little.
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 43] Then, calling his disciples to him, Jesus said, ‘I tell you that this poor widow has put in more than all the others who were putting money into the chests;
[OEB | EN | NT | Mark | Chapter 12 | Verse 44] for everyone else put in something from what he had to spare, while she, in her need, put in all she had – everything that she had to live on.’
