[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 1] When the uproar had ceased, Paul sent for the disciples, and, with encouraging words, bade them goodbye, and started on his journey to Macedonia.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 2] After going through those districts and speaking many encouraging words to the disciples, he went into Greece, where he stayed three months.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 3] He was about to sail to Syria, when he learned that a plot had been laid against him by several of the Jewish leaders; so he decided to return by way of Macedonia.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 4] He was accompanied by Sopater the son of Pyrrhus, of Beroea, Aristarchus and Secundus from Thessalonica, Gaius of Derbe, and Timothy, as well as by Tychicus and Trophimus of Roman Asia.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 5] These people went to Troas and waited for us there;
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 6] while we ourselves sailed from Philippi after the Passover, and joined them five days later at Troas, where we stayed for a week.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 7] On the first day of the week, when we had met for the breaking of bread, Paul, who was intending to leave the next day, began to address those who were present, and prolonged his address until midnight.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 8] There were a good many lamps in the upstairs room, where we had met;
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 9] and a young man named Eutychus, sitting at the window, was gradually overcome with great drowsiness, as Paul continued his address. At last, quite overpowered by his drowsiness, he fell from the third story to the ground, and was picked up for dead.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 10] But Paul went down, threw himself on him, and put his arms round him. ‘Do not be alarmed,’ he said, ‘he is still alive.’
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 11] Then he went upstairs; and, after breaking and partaking of the bread, he talked with them at great length until daybreak, and then left.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 12] Meanwhile they had taken the lad away alive, and were greatly comforted.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 13] We started first, went on board ship, and sailed for Assos, intending to take Paul on board there. This was by his own arrangement, as he intended to go by land himself.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 14] So, when he met us at Assos, we took him on board and went on to Mitylene.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 15] The day after we had sailed from there, we arrived off Chios, touched at Samos the following day, and the next day reached Miletus;
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 16] for Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus, so as to avoid spending much time in Roman Asia. He was making haste to reach Jerusalem, if possible, by the Festival at the close of the Harvest.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 17] From Miletus, however, he sent to Ephesus and invited the church elders to meet him;
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 18] and, when they came, he said to them, ‘You know well the life that I always led among you from the very first day that I set foot in Roman Asia,
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 19] serving the Lord, as I did, in all humility, amid the tears and trials which fell to my lot through the plots of some of the Jewish leaders.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 20] I never shrank from telling you anything that could be helpful to you, or from teaching you both in public and in private.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 21] I earnestly pointed both Jews and Greeks to the repentance that leads to God, and to faith in Jesus, our Lord.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 22] And now, under spiritual constraint, I am here on my way to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there,
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 23] except that in town after town the Holy Spirit plainly declares to me that imprisonment and troubles await me.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 24] But I count my life of no value to myself, if only I may complete the course marked out for me, and the task that was allotted me by the Lord Jesus – which was to declare the good news of the love of God.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 25] And now, I tell you, I know that none of you will ever see my face again – you among whom I have gone about proclaiming the kingdom.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 26] Therefore I declare to you this day, that my conscience is clear in regard to the fate of any of you,
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 27] for I have not shrunk from announcing the whole purpose of God regarding you.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 28] Be watchful over yourselves, and over the whole flock, of which the Holy Spirit has placed you in charge, to shepherd the church of God, which he won for himself at the cost of his life.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 29] I know that, after my departure, merciless wolves will get in among you, who will not spare the flock;
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 30] and from among yourselves, too, people will arise, who will teach perversions of truth, so as to draw away the disciples after them.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 31] Therefore, be on your guard, remembering how for three years, night and day, I never ceased, even with tears, to warn each one of you.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 32] And now I commend you to the Lord and to the message of his love – a message which has the power to build up your characters, and to give you your place among all those who have become Christ’s people.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 33] I have never coveted anyone’s gold or silver or clothing.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 34] You, yourselves, know that these hands of mine provided not only for my own wants, but for my companions also.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 35] I left nothing undone to show you that, labouring as I laboured, you ought to help the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said himself – “It is more blessed to give than to receive.”’
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 36] When Paul had finished speaking, he knelt down and prayed with them all.
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 37] All were in tears; and throwing their arms round Paul’s neck, they kissed him again and again,
[OEB | EN | NT | Acts | Chapter 20 | Verse 38] grieving most of all over what he had said – that they would never see his face again. Then they escorted him to the ship.
